Reliability

G-Series gearboxes: built for reliability and longevity

In demanding industrial environments reliability is paramount. Here is what goes into a G-Series gearbox to make it last, and what keeps it lasting once it is installed.

By the Gishnu Gears engineering teamUpdated 23 November 20262 min read

In demanding industrial environments, reliability is paramount. A gearbox sits between the motor and the work, and when it stops, the machine stops with it. Gishnu G-Series gearboxes are engineered with durability in mind.

Key features for reliability

High-quality materials

G-Series gearboxes are constructed from materials such as cast iron and steel. The housing has to hold the shafts and bearings in position under load for years, and these materials give it the strength to do so.

Precision manufacturing

Consistent performance comes from consistent parts. Gears are cut and finished on dedicated machines, and the results are measured in-house. Our infrastructure includes CNC gear hobbing, worm grinding, a gear measuring centre and a coordinate measuring machine, backed by rigorous quality control.

Robust design

Robust bearings, strong shafts and effective lubrication are what carry a gearbox through shock loads and long shifts. Together they ensure long service life and minimise downtime.

Getting the service life the gearbox was built for

A well-made gearbox still depends on how it is selected, installed and looked after. Always follow the instructions supplied with your unit. The points below are general good practice for worm gearboxes.

  • Size it for the real duty. Shock loads, long running hours and frequent starts all call for a margin above the calculated torque. Our selection guide covers the steps.
  • Mount it on a rigid, level base. A base that flexes puts the shafts out of line and loads the bearings.
  • Align the couplings. Misalignment between motor, gearbox and driven shaft is a common cause of early bearing and seal wear.
  • Keep the oil right. Use the recommended grade, keep it at the correct level and change it at the recommended intervals.
  • Let it breathe. Keep the breather clear so pressure does not build up and push oil past the seals.
  • Keep the housing clean. The fins on the housing shed heat. A layer of dust or product keeps it in.

Signs worth acting on

A change in the way a gearbox sounds or feels is usually the first warning. Investigate if you notice a rise in running temperature, new noise or vibration, oil at the shaft seals, or oil that has darkened or carries metal particles. Attending to these early is far cheaper than an unplanned stop.
